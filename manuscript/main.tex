\begin{abstract}
Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus (RAPM) estimates player associations with
scoring margin from stint-level lineup indicators. When low-minute player
columns are removed while their stints remain in the sample, the reduced
design no longer records the complete lineup. We introduce Dummy RAPM, which
augments the retained-player matrix with five low-minute-player count
indicators for each lineup side. The ten indicators receive a common ridge
penalty distinct from the player penalty. We evaluate the model over
\NumberSeasons{} NBA seasons using chronological inner validation and an
untouched March--April test period. Inner validation selects a
\SelectedThreshold-minute-per-appearance threshold and
a dummy-to-player penalty ratio of \SelectedPenaltyRatio. Dummy RAPM lowers
mean game-margin RMSE from \MeanNoDummyRMSE{} to \MeanDummyRMSE{}, an average
gain of \MeanRMSEGain{} points (\RelativeRMSEGain), and improves RMSE in
\RMSEWins{} of \NumberSeasons{} seasons. The gain is modest in magnitude but
consistent across paired season-level tests. The results support a
partially pooled representation of lineup-side counts, while providing little
evidence that filtering players without representing them improves
prediction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Basic plus-minus measures a team's score differential while a player is on the
court but does not adjust for teammates or opponents. Adjusted Plus-Minus
(APM), associated with the WINVAL work of Jeff Sagarin and Wayne Winston and
described publicly by \citet{Rosenbaum2004}, addresses this limitation by
regressing stint-level score differentials on player indicators.

APM estimates are unstable when players repeatedly share the floor with the
same teammates and opponents or appear in few stints. Ridge regression
stabilizes correlated designs by shrinking coefficients
\citep{HoerlKennard1970}. \citet{Sill2010} applied this approach to APM and
emphasized evaluation on future games, yielding Regularized Adjusted
Plus-Minus (RAPM). Bayesian partial-pooling approaches provide a related
response to weak identification.
\citet{FearnheadTaylor2011} estimated offensive and defensive abilities while
borrowing information across seasons, and \citet{DeshpandeJensen2016} modeled
changes in win probability using Bayesian linear regression. Box Plus-Minus
(BPM) is a separate box-score-based branch rather than a Bayesian plus-minus
estimator itself. However, the BPM 2.0 regression was trained against
Bayesian-prior-informed, multiyear RAPM targets \citep{Myers2020}.

Some RAPM implementations remove low-minute players before fitting. Removing a
column does not remove the corresponding stints from the response, so the
omitted contribution remains in the response. It can be attributed to
retained-player coefficients when it is correlated with retained-player
indicators; otherwise it is absorbed primarily by the intercept and residual.
We ask whether a small set of partially pooled count indicators can represent
this missing lineup information without estimating an individual coefficient
for every low-minute player.

Our contribution is methodological and empirical. We construct separate count
indicators for excluded home and away players, assign them a ridge penalty
distinct from the player penalty, and evaluate the resulting model using
chronological nested selection. We also impose explicit lineup, scoring, and
game-margin reconstruction checks before fitting.

\section{Model}

\subsection{Stint response and player design}

A stint is an interval over which neither lineup changes. Let $\Delta_i$ be the
change in home-minus-away score margin during stint $i$, and define exposure
\[
q_i = \frac{P_{H,i}+P_{A,i}}{2},
\]
where $P_{H,i}$ and $P_{A,i}$ count home and away possession-ending events.
The response is
\[
y_i = 100\frac{\Delta_i}{q_i},
\]
the home-team margin per 100 team possessions. Observation $i$ receives weight
$q_i$, so a longer stint contributes more to estimation. Scaling before
estimation makes coefficients directly interpretable in points per 100 team
possessions.

For retained player $j$, $x_{ij}=1$ when the player is in the home lineup,
$x_{ij}=-1$ when in the away lineup, and $x_{ij}=0$ otherwise. The ordinary
no-dummy RAPM estimator is proportional to
\[
\left(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)
=
\arg\min_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{\beta}}
\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_i
\left(y_i-\alpha-\mathbf{x}_i^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)^2
+\lambda_P\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2
\right\}.
\]
The intercept is not penalized, and predictors are not standardized. Models
are fit with \texttt{glmnet} \citep{FriedmanHastieTibshirani2010}.

\subsection{Lineup-side count indicators}

For $k=1,\ldots,5$, define
\[
d_{H,i}^{(k)}
=
\mathbb{1}\{\text{the home lineup in stint }i
\text{ contains exactly }k\text{ excluded players}\},
\]
\[
d_{A,i}^{(k)}
=
\mathbb{1}\{\text{the away lineup in stint }i
\text{ contains exactly }k\text{ excluded players}\}.
\]
Count zero is the reference category. The augmented model therefore contains
ten dummy regressors, five for each lineup side. Separate player, home-dummy,
and away-dummy namespaces prevent numeric player identifiers from colliding
with dummy names. A five-column signed encoding would impose equal-and-opposite
home and away coefficients at each count. The ten-column encoding does not
impose that restriction: a given number of excluded home players and excluded
away players can have different magnitudes rather than being forced to differ
only in sign.

Let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_H$ and $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_A$ denote the two
five-element dummy blocks. Dummy RAPM estimates
\[
\begin{split}
\arg\min_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{\beta},
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_H,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_A}
\quad&
\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_i
\left(
y_i-\alpha-\mathbf{x}_i^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\beta}
-\mathbf{d}_{H,i}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_H
-\mathbf{d}_{A,i}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_A
\right)^2\\
&+\lambda_P\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2
+\lambda_D\left(
\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_H\|_2^2+
\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_A\|_2^2
\right).
\end{split}
\]
We tune the relative penalty $\rho=\lambda_D/\lambda_P$. A common
dummy-specific penalty permits a different degree of pooling from player
coefficients without introducing ten sparsely estimated hyperparameters.

\subsection{Low-minute threshold}

\citet{Rosenbaum2004} placed players with less than 250 minutes in the two
seasons combined into a common reference category. Our threshold instead uses
mean minutes conditional on appearing in a game, calculated only from the
relevant training period. This avoids labeling an ordinary rotation player as
low-minute solely because of missed games, but it differs from a total-minutes
rule and can retain players with few appearances. We therefore interpret the
threshold as a prediction-tuned modeling choice rather than a definition of
player quality.

\section{Data Construction and Validation}

We obtain publicly accessible ESPN play-by-play and player box-score records
through the open-source \texttt{hoopR} package \citep{Gilani2026}. Official
box-score starter flags initialize each five-player lineup; recorded
substitutions update the lineup thereafter. If either team lacks exactly five
unique recorded starters, we exclude the full game rather than guess an
opening lineup. Across the primary sample, \StarterExcludedGames{} games are
excluded for this reason, including
\StarterExcludedTwentyNineteenGames{} in 2019.

Every modeled stint must contain exactly five unique home players and five
unique away players. Stint points are constructed from changes in the reported
home-minus-away scoreboard margin, including the first scoring event in each
stint. This scoreboard-delta construction credits technical free throws and
other free-throw points even when an event is not marked as ending a
possession. A scoring segment with zero possession exposure---for example, free
throws separated by a substitution---is joined to the next positive-exposure
stint, or to the preceding one at the end of a game. Thus no scored point is
assigned an infinite per-possession response. The reconstructed stint margins
must sum exactly to the official final margin in every included game.
Period labels are also required to form a contiguous sequence beginning at
one; this removes a single disconnected postgame record labeled as period 29
without truncating valid multi-overtime games.

The primary analysis retains all possessions, including those labeled as
garbage time and those played in overtime. Removing them would change the
full-game prediction target. The analysis uses open-source software, but we
describe the underlying ESPN data as publicly accessible rather than fully
open source.

Table~\ref{tab:sample} reports the season-level sample. The analysis contains
\TotalModeledGames{} games and \TotalModeledStints{} positive-exposure stints.
``Out'' denotes regular-season games in May--September, which are excluded
before model fitting or evaluation. Starter exclusions may overlap the
out-of-window count.

\section{Selection and Evaluation}

The sample contains \NumberSeasons{} seasons: 2007--2019 and 2022--2025. It
begins in 2007 because starter metadata are incomplete in earlier seasons.
The disrupted 2019--20 season and the COVID-shortened 2020--21 season are
excluded because neither supports the same October--April chronological split.

Within each season, October--December games form the inner training period,
January--February games form the inner validation period, and March--April
games form the outer test period. Outer training is exactly the union of the
October--December and January--February windows. May--September games are in
none of the four analysis sets. Programmatic checks require every game to
receive one disjoint base-window label and require the latest training date to
precede the earliest validation or test date.

The initial inner search considers
average-minutes-per-appearance thresholds
\[
\{0,5,10,15,20,25,30\}
\]
and dummy-to-player penalty ratios
\[
\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2\}.
\]
At threshold zero, all players observed in the relevant training period are
retained. The augmented fit is implemented by reusing the no-dummy fit and
appending ten exactly zero dummy coefficients. Its predictions, selected
lambda, RMSE, and $R^2$ are therefore required to equal the no-dummy quantities
exactly. It remains a training-observed baseline rather than a claim that every
possible player is known in advance.

The broad grid selects a \SelectedThreshold-minute threshold. We then evaluate
the following penalty grid at that threshold:
\[
\rho\in\{1.0,1.1,\ldots,2.5\}.
\]
If a boundary value wins, the grid is extended by increments of 0.1 until the
optimum is interior. The checked grid runs from
\CheckedPenaltyMinimum{} to \CheckedPenaltyMaximum{} and selects
$\rho=\SelectedPenaltyRatio$.
All selection uses mean inner-validation game-margin RMSE across seasons. To
avoid selecting a threshold for only one member of the final comparison, the
threshold score averages Dummy RAPM and no-dummy RAPM RMSE. Exact ties prefer
the lower minute threshold and then the larger dummy penalty. Neither outer
RMSE nor $R^2$ participates in selection.

After selection, both models are refit using all games through February and
evaluated once on March--April games. Player eligibility is recomputed using
only the corresponding training period. One deterministic five-fold,
game-grouped assignment is constructed for each season and training window,
using a seed based only on season and window. The same assignment is passed to
every threshold, model, and penalty ratio. Within each fit, $\lambda_P$ is
chosen by minimizing cross-validated aggregated game-margin RMSE. Grouping
prevents stints from the same game from entering both sides of a fold; common
folds ensure that candidate specifications face the same validation split.

For game $g$, stint predictions are converted back to points and summed:
\[
\widehat{\Delta}_g
=
\sum_{i\in g}\widehat{y}_i q_i/100.
\]
RMSE is the selection and headline metric. We also report descriptive
out-of-sample
\[
R^2_{\mathrm{oos}}
=
1-\frac{\sum_{g\in\mathcal{T}}
(\Delta_g-\widehat{\Delta}_g)^2}
{\sum_{g\in\mathcal{T}}
(\Delta_g-\overline{\Delta}_{\mathcal{T}})^2},
\]
where $\mathcal{T}$ is the March--April test set and the comparator predicts
that season's mean test-set margin for every game. Paired season-level RMSE
differences are summarized with two-sided paired $t$, Wilcoxon signed-rank, and
sign tests. These are descriptive comparisons across seasons, not independent
experimental replications.

The exercise conditions on the realized held-out lineups. It evaluates
out-of-sample lineup prediction and should not be interpreted as a pregame
forecast.

with its small absolute magnitude rather than as evidence of a large practical
effect.

\subsection{Filtering and representation}

The result does not imply that filtering alone improves RAPM. Relative to the
zero-threshold no-dummy baseline, the selected no-dummy model is worse by
\NoDummyBaselineRMSELoss{} RMSE on average
($p=\NoDummyBaselinePValue$). Dummy RAPM improves on that baseline by
\DummyBaselineRMSEGain{} RMSE, but this broader comparison is less precise
($p=\DummyBaselinePValue$). The stronger finding is the controlled comparison
at the same selected threshold: representing excluded-player counts performs
better than simply dropping those columns.

The dummy coefficients summarize regularized lineup-level associations and do
not estimate causal effects for excluded players. Low-minute lineups may be
selected in response to score state, injuries, matchups, or schedule
conditions. Relative to zero excluded players on the same side, one excluded
home player is associated with \HomeOneCoefficient{} home-margin points per

The coefficients are not monotone in the count. Counts of four and five are
exactly zero in many seasons, indicating rare categories that are strongly
pooled toward zero. We therefore do not interpret the dummy sequence as a
dose-response curve, and the cross-season intervals in

not classical coefficient confidence intervals.

\section{Discussion}

The analysis supports a narrow conclusion: after selecting a
\SelectedThreshold-minute threshold without using the outer test period,
adding ten lineup-side count indicators lowers mean held-out RMSE relative to
dropping the same player columns without representation. The focused inner
search favors stronger shrinkage for dummy coefficients than for player
coefficients. The exact ordering is shallow across the focused grid, and an
arbitrarily large dummy penalty would converge toward the no-dummy model.

Several limitations matter. First, the result concerns game-margin prediction,
not causal player effects. Second, lineups are reconstructed from event data
and starter records rather than an official shift chart. The lineup and
score-reconciliation checks catch many errors, but a valid-looking lineup can
still be incorrect. Third, the exact penalty optimum is shallow;
$\rho=\SelectedPenaltyRatio$ is a reproducible grid choice rather than a
sharply identified scientific constant. Fourth, starter metadata require game
exclusions concentrated in 2019, and high dummy counts are rare. Finally,
adjacent season-level comparisons share players, coaches, and league
conditions. The paired tests do not remove this temporal dependence, and
future-season validation remains valuable.

\section{Conclusion}

Dummy RAPM offers a compact way to represent low-minute players whose
individual columns are omitted from a RAPM design. In the selected
specification, five home and five away count indicators receive a common
penalty \SelectedPenaltyRatio{} times the player penalty. Across
\NumberSeasons{} seasons, the model improves game-margin RMSE in \RMSEWins{}
seasons. The average gain is modest, and $R^2$ is retained only as a secondary
description. The evidence favors partially pooling omitted-player counts, not
filtering players by itself.
